What we do

Remodeling, start to finish.

One crew, one point of contact, one written plan. From the first walkthrough to the final clean-up, you know who is on your property and what happens next.

  • Exteriors

    Siding, windows, doors, trim, decks and porches — the outside of the house, built to stand up to Spokane winters. We don't do roofs.

  • Flooring

    Hardwood, laminate, vinyl plank and tile. Old floors out, subfloor fixed, new floors laid flat and tight.

  • Basement finishes

    Turn unfinished square footage into bedrooms, a family room or a shop — framed, insulated and finished to code.

  • Demolition

    Interior tear-outs, deck and shed removal, and site clean-up. Done safely, hauled away, swept clean.

How we work

No shortcuts. No surprises.

Remodeling is hard on a household. The job of a good contractor is to make it predictable. Here is what you can hold us to.

  • Straight talk

    A written scope and a written price before any work starts. If something changes, you hear it from us first — in writing, with the number attached.

  • We show up

    A schedule you can plan your life around. The crew arrives when we said it would and works the full day.

  • Done right

    Built to code, built square and built to last. We would rather do it once and do it properly than come back to fix it.

  • Your home, respected

    Dust walls up, floors protected, tools put away, site swept at the end of every day. You are still living here.

The process

Four steps. One crew.

  1. 01

    Walkthrough

    We come to the house, look at the space, and talk through what you want and what the structure will allow.

  2. 02

    Written estimate

    A line-item scope and price, with the materials and timeline spelled out. No vague allowances.

  3. 03

    The build

    Demo, rough-in, inspections, finish work. You get a regular update and a single person to call.

  4. 04

    Final walkthrough

    We walk every inch of it with you before we call it done. Anything that is not right gets fixed.
